The Scaling Hypothesis Is Language-Contingent

Evidence from Cross-Linguistic Training Dynamics

Adam Zachary Wasserman, Open Honest Foundation. Preprint, 4 April 2026.

Download the PDF

Abstract

The scaling hypothesis holds that language model performance improves predictably with compute, data and parameters, following power laws that are assumed to be universal.

This paper tests that assumption with a pre-registered controlled ablation, training identical 125M-parameter transformers on matched English and French corpora. Holding architecture, parameter count and token budget constant leaves language as the manipulated variable.

The scaling behaviour differs by language, which means the universality assumption is doing work it has not earned. A French edition of the paper is deposited alongside the English one.
