Finite Testability of Enterprise Software

A Quantitative Survey of Mutable State Across 200 Public Open-Source Codebases

Adam Zachary Wasserman, Open Honest Foundation. Preprint, 25 May 2026. Paper 1 of the Open Honest empirical research programme.

Download the PDF

Abstract

Most enterprise codebases cannot be exhaustively verified, and this is a structural property rather than a matter of effort or discipline. This paper measures one indicator of that property, the mutable-state ratio, across 200 public open-source repositories and roughly 2.2 million functions in four languages.

The median codebase sits at 61.1% in Python, 53.0% in Java, and 40.0% in C#. Only 1 of 175 non-frontend codebases analysed falls below the structural-testability threshold. TypeScript is bimodal, with a React frontend subpopulation under 15% and a class-based backend subpopulation far above it, which suggests the driver is paradigm rather than language.

The analyser, a 249-scenario behavioural specification, and the corpus seeds are released alongside the paper, so any third party can reproduce every figure.
